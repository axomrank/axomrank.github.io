# Curriculum: all of Assam's history

The History subject is one continuous path of courses from earliest Assam to today. Each course follows [LESSON-DESIGN.md](LESSON-DESIGN.md): eras → lessons → units, with a checkpoint at the end of every era. Tiers (A/B/C) set how long a lesson is; the exam weight decides the tier. Titles below are working names; dates are to be verified against sources when each lesson is written (every lesson lists its `sources`).

## H1 · Early Assam: Pragjyotisha and Kamarupa
- **Era 1 Sources and legends:** Kalika Purana, Yogini Tantra, Mahabharata links; Pragjyotisha, Kamarupa, the name Assam. Stone and copper-plate inscriptions as sources.
- **Era 2 Early dynasties:** Danava, Naraka, Bhagadatta; the Varmans (Pushyavarman, Bhaskaravarman, Harshavardhana, Xuanzang's visit c. 643); Salastambha dynasty; Pala dynasty of Kamarupa (Brahmapala, Ratnapala, Indrapala, Dharmapala).
- **Era 3 End of Kamarupa:** later Pala successors, Kamarupa–Gauda–Pala conflicts, Vaidyadeva, the Mlechchha chiefs; Bakhtiyar Khalji's failed march (1206).
- Maps: Kamarupa's boundaries (Karatoya to Dikkarvasini), capitals Pragjyotishpura, Haruppeswara, Durjaya.

## H2 · Kamata, Koch and neighbours
- **Era 1 Kamata and the Baro-Bhuyans:** Sandhya, Kamateswar rulers; Khen dynasty (Niladhwaj, Chakradhwaj, Nilambar); Alauddin Hussain Shah's conquest (c. 1498); the Baro-Bhuyans.
- **Era 2 The Koch kingdom:** Biswa Singha, Nara Narayan and Chilarai (Sukladhwaj), split into Koch Bihar and Koch Hajo, Raghudev, Parikshit, Mughal relations.
- **Era 3 Neighbouring kingdoms:** Chutiya kingdom (Sadiya), Kachari/Dimasa kingdom (Dimapur, Maibong, Khaspur), Jaintia kingdom, Tripura and Manipur contacts, the Borahi and Moran/Matak peoples.

## H3 · The Ahom Kings (done)
Five eras, 41 lessons: founding kings (1228–1497), expansion (to Susenphaa), the Mughal wars (Saraighat 1671), the Tungkhungia kings (Rudra Singha to Lakshmi Singha), and decline. Planned improvements: more exam questions per lesson, previous-year tags, maps for each reign.

## H4 · Wars of the Ahom age by theme
For fast revision across reigns: Mughal–Ahom wars (Mir Jumla 1662–63, Ramsingh and Lachit Borphukan, Saraighat, Itakhuli 1682); the Moamoria rebellion; the Burmese invasions (1817–1826); the Treaty of Yandabo (1826); administration (Paik system, Khel, Barua, Phukan, Borbarua, Bargohain, Buragohain, Satra land).

## H5 · British period and freedom struggle (1826–1947)
- **Era 1 Annexation:** Yandabo, Purandar Singha's upper Assam, Maniram Dewan, the 1857 link, Kandarpeswar Singha; the tea industry and the opening of the Assam Company; the Phulaguri uprising (1861); Patharughat (1894).
- **Era 2 Administration:** Assam as part of Bengal, Chief Commissionership (1874), Eastern Bengal and Assam (1905), Province of Assam (1912), Legislative Council, Government of India Acts 1919 and 1935, the Line System, Sylhet.
- **Era 3 Awakening:** Assamese language revival (Orunodoi, Jonaki, Chandrakumar Agarwala, Lakshminath Bezbaroa, Hemchandra Barua), Assam Association (1903), the Assam Sahitya Sabha (1917).
- **Era 4 Freedom movement:** Non-Cooperation (Gandhi in Assam, 1921), Kanaklata Barua, Kushal Konwar, Quit India (1942), Bhogeswari Phukanani, the INA, Gopinath Bordoloi, Cabinet Mission and Grouping, Sylhet referendum (1947).
- **Hill resistance:** Khasi (U Tirot Sing), Garo, Naga, Mizo (Lushai) risings, Jaintia, Singpho, Khamti, Kuki.

## H6 · Assam since 1947
Integration and reorganisation (Nagaland 1963, Meghalaya 1972, Mizoram and Arunachal 1987); the 1960 language movement and 1972 Assamese-medium issue; the Assam Movement (1979–85), the Assam Accord (1985), AGP; Bodo movement, Bodoland Territorial Council (2003, Sixth Schedule), Karbi and Dima Hasao councils; insurgency and peace accords; NRC and CAA; people: Gopinath Bordoloi, Bishnuram Medhi, Bimala Prasad Chaliha and later chief ministers.

## H7 · Religion, literature, art, culture
Sankardev and Madhavdev, Neo-Vaishnavism, satras and naam-ghars (Majuli, Barpeta, Auniati); Shakta and Tantric traditions (Kamakhya); the Charyapada, Hema Saraswati, Ananta Kandali, Ramayana translations, Buranjis; Bihu, Ojapali, Bhaona, Sattriya, Borgeet; weaving, silk (muga, eri, pat), bamboo and cane; Jyoti Prasad Agarwala and Bishnu Rabha; Jnanpith winners; Assamese language history.

## H8 · Sources, monuments and archaeology
Inscriptions (Dubi, Nidhanpur, Doobi copper plates; Rock edicts), Ambari, Da Parbatia, Madan Kamdev, Hajo, Sibsagar group (Rang Ghar, Talatal Ghar, Charaideo moidams), Gargaon, Kareng Ghar, Kaziranga region; chronicle sources; foreign accounts (Xuanzang, Minhaj-i-Siraj, Shihabuddin Talish, Mirza Nathan).

## H9 · Peoples and councils
Bodo-Kachari, Karbi, Dimasa, Mising, Rabha, Tiwa, Deori, Sonowal, Tai peoples, Tea-tribe communities; Sixth Schedule and autonomous councils; Inner Line, Line System, tribal belts and blocks.

## Content rules for every lesson
- Facts need a named source in `sources`; flag disputed dates in the lesson.
- Spelling of names follows official/most common exam usage; keep alternates in `entities.json` aliases.
- Every lesson adds to the shared `entities.json` (people, places, offices, terms) and the master timeline.
- Every question carries `exams` tags (adre, tet, apsc, si) and, if from a past paper, `pyq` with year.

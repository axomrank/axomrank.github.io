# Kamarupa: textual geography in the sources lesson

Research 2026-11-03. The lesson diagram is a newly drawn schematic, not a historical map or evidence of a surveyed political frontier. It does not assign coordinates, equate Diksu with Dikhu or Dikkaravasini with Dikrang, or convert yojanas into kilometres. It follows the user's sketch in presenting a triangle, but does not identify Kanjagiri with a particular modern mountain or the southern confluence with Navālaya.

- Kālikā Purāṇa 50.76–77, B. N. Shastri / Surendra Pratap translation, local `Primary source/Kalika-Purana-B-N-Shastri-Surendra-Pratap-2008.txt`, lines 23132–23146, printed p. 353: Karatoyā to Dikkaravāsinī, triangular, 100 yojanas long, 30 broad. In 38.118 and 127 (printed pp. 245–246), a distinct narrative instead names Lalitakāntā as the eastern reference; it should not be silently identified with Dikkaravāsinī.
- Yoginī Tantra I.11.16–18, 21–23, local `Primary source/Yogini-Tantra-English-Working-Translation-2026.txt`, lines 2786–2815, a provisional translation of the Mishra 1984 edition: Karatoyā to Dikkaravāsinī; north Kañjagiri, west Karatoyā, east Dikṣu, south Brahmaputra–Lākṣā confluence, triangle and same dimensions. Names and interpretations remain uncertain. The working translation cautions that Girikanyake is a vocative, not a boundary. Its nine sacred categories in 11.24–26 should not be silently replaced with a four-pitha territorial scheme.
- B. Kakati, [The Mother Goddess Kamakhya](https://shivashakti.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/kamakhya.pdf), printed pp. 7–8: reports a later Haragaurīsaṃvāda scheme of Ratna, Kāma, Svarṇa and Saumāra, and a variant enumerating Kāma, Ratna, Bhadra and Saumāra. He distinguishes these from the Yoginī Tantra's nine-seat account. This is a secondary report; we have not independently collated Haragaurīsaṃvāda. Accordingly, the user's proposed assignment of one four-pitha list to each of the two texts is not taught as settled fact.

# English course unit map

This is the authoring order for the 15 lessons and 75 units in the
[English course plan](ENGLISH-COURSE.md). Each numbered entry is a unit.
All 15 lessons and 75 units are drafted. The course remains outside the catalog pending review. All learner-facing course copy is in English.

## Part 1 · Words

### 1. Near-synonyms in context · 6 units

1. **Make the door sign.** Choose *quiet* or *silent* from sound, speech and street activity clues.
2. **Describe the announcer.** Separate *quiet* behaviour from *calm* feelings using a quiet but nervous speaker and a calm speaker in a noisy room.
3. **Set the walking pace.** Distinguish *quick*, *fast* and *rapid* using response time, movement and change.
4. **Write the damage report.** Distinguish *broken*, *damaged* and *faulty* using an object's condition and whether it works.
5. **Choose the message tone.** Compare *ask*, *request* and *demand* using urgency, authority and wording.
6. **Edit the noticeboard.** Mix earlier families with new sentences. Identify the clue before selecting a replacement.

### 2. Idioms · 6 units

1. **Start the group chat.** Infer *break the ice* from a first meeting.
2. **Save the deadline.** Use *at the last minute*, *in the nick of time* and *against the clock* in distinct time situations.
3. **Explain the mix-up.** Read *get the wrong end of the stick* and *jump to conclusions* from what a person misunderstood or assumed.
4. **React to the result.** Infer *over the moon*, *under the weather* and *down in the dumps* from behaviour and context.
5. **Keep the surprise.** Distinguish *spill the beans*, *let the cat out of the bag* and *keep something under wraps*.
6. **Rewrite the chat.** Match idioms to plain-English paraphrases and spot one literal reading in a new conversation.

### 3. Spelling · 6 units

1. **Fix two swapped letters.** Use *quiet/quite* in sentences before reconstructing the spelling.
2. **Build the ending.** Contrast *hope → hoping* and *hop → hopping*.
3. **Send the invitation.** Distinguish *their/there/they’re* using possession, place and contraction.
4. **Pack for the trip.** Rebuild words with silent letters such as *knee*, *write* and *climb*.
5. **Proof the notice.** Use families such as *receive/reception* and practise a short set of commonly misspelt words individually.
6. **Edit a short message.** Choose and repair errors across the earlier patterns. Accept standard spelling variants where the prompt allows them.

### 4. One-word substitutions · 6 units

1. **Label the bookshelf.** Sort *biography*, *autobiography* and *memoir* by author and scope.
2. **Staff the event.** Choose roles such as *volunteer*, *spectator* and *participant* from what people do.
3. **Name the place.** Match *archive*, *library* and *museum* to what each preserves or provides.
4. **Name the group.** Use group nouns in conventional combinations such as a *fleet* of ships and a *herd* of cattle.
5. **Describe how often.** Contrast *annual*, *biennial* and *occasional* using calendars and explicit intervals.
6. **Shorten an announcement.** Replace phrases with precise terms and explain when a near-miss changes the claim.

### 5. Words from context · 4 units

1. **Find the example clue.** Infer a category from examples introduced by *such as*.
2. **Find the contrast clue.** Read what changes after *but*, *unlike* or *whereas*.
3. **Choose the right sense.** Contrast uses of *charge*, *light* and *book* using the surrounding words.
4. **Check the paraphrase.** Substitute a candidate meaning, then check that the sentence still makes sense.

## Part 2 · Grammar

### 6. Prepositions · 6 units

1. **Mark the meeting time.** Use *at*, *on* and *in* for clock times, days and longer periods.
2. **Pin the meeting place.** Contrast a point, a surface and an enclosed place.
3. **Follow the route.** Distinguish *across*, *through*, *into* and *along* on a simple diagram.
4. **Describe the wait.** Contrast *since*, *for*, *until* and *by* using start, duration, continuation and deadline.
5. **Pair the words.** Practise fixed pairings such as *interested in*, *depend on* and *responsible for*.
6. **Repair the invitation.** Mix time, place, movement and fixed pairs in a short message.

### 7. Articles · 4 units

1. **Listen to the first sound.** Choose *a/an* with *hour*, *uniform* and familiar controls.
2. **Introduce and point back.** Use *a/an* on introduction and *the* when the reader knows which item.
3. **Speak generally.** Compare bare plural and mass nouns with specific groups or amounts.
4. **Edit the directions.** Mix sound and reference cues in a note with first and later mentions.

### 8. Subject–verb agreement · 4 units

1. **Find who does it.** Identify the head of the subject before choosing the verb.
2. **Ignore the nearby noun.** Work through phrases such as *the box of pencils*.
3. **Count joined subjects.** Contrast two subjects joined by *and* with one subject plus extra information.
4. **Check difficult subjects.** Apply agreement to *each*, *everyone* and *there is/are* using clear cases.

### 9. Verbs and time · 4 units

1. **Place the event on a timeline.** Contrast routine, completed past action and plans with explicit time cues.
2. **Show a scene in progress.** Use progressive forms for actions ongoing at a stated time.
3. **Connect past to now.** Contrast past simple with present perfect in clearly specified contexts.
4. **Keep the story consistent.** Repair a short account with foreground events, background action and time markers.

### 10. Comparison and description · 4 units

1. **Compare two routes.** Choose comparative forms and use *than*.
2. **Choose from a group.** Use superlatives and explicit comparison sets.
3. **Compare without ranking.** Practise *as … as* and irregular forms such as *better/best*.
4. **Describe the action.** Distinguish adjective and adverb roles and repair double comparatives.

### 11. Sentence transformations · 4 units

1. **Check the tag.** Match polarity, auxiliary and pronoun in basic question tags.
2. **Pass on a message.** Track speaker and time when turning direct speech into reported speech.
3. **Change the focus.** Rewrite a straightforward active sentence in the passive and preserve tense.
4. **Check what survived.** Compare originals with transformed sentences for meaning, time and reference.

### 12. Blanks and punctuation · 6 units

1. **Join ideas.** Choose connectors for addition, contrast, cause and result.
2. **Track the reference.** Resolve pronouns by checking what they can refer to.
3. **Separate sentences.** Repair fragments and run-ons in short notices.
4. **Mark possession or omission.** Distinguish plural forms, possessives and contractions.
5. **Show the speaker.** Use quotation boundaries and punctuation consistently in a short dialogue.
6. **Complete a short message.** Mix connectors, grammar and punctuation using clues on both sides of each blank.

## Part 3 · Use

### 13. Error spotting and correction · 6 units

1. **Find the smallest repair.** Tap the error and change only what the sentence needs.
2. **Trace the subject.** Revisit agreement with an intervening phrase.
3. **Trace the time.** Fix a verb form using an explicit time marker.
4. **Check the small words.** Correct article and preposition errors using earlier rules.
5. **Check comparisons and pronouns.** Repair an unclear reference or malformed comparison.
6. **Decide whether to edit.** Mix correct sentences with sentences containing a single clear error.

### 14. Sentence arrangement · 3 units

1. **Find the opening.** Put an introduction before a sentence that refers back to it.
2. **Link the middle.** Use time, cause and contrast markers to order sentences.
3. **Prove the order.** Arrange a short paragraph and identify the clue that rules out a tempting swap.

### 15. Reading comprehension · 6 units

Write two original passages per unit. Use one for guided practice and the other for transfer.

1. **Find the stated detail.** Use a club notice and a timetable update.
2. **Put events in order.** Use a missed-bus story and an account of preparing for a school fair.
3. **Infer a word from the text.** Use a library announcement and a market scene.
4. **Make a supported inference.** Use a short dialogue and an account of a lost item.
5. **Choose the main idea and purpose.** Use an opinion paragraph and a community information leaflet.
6. **Read under mixed conditions.** Use a short explanatory article and a letter. Combine detail, inference and purpose with evidence for every answer.

## Authoring and release sequence

1. Finish Lesson 1 as a draft. Use a concrete decision, feedback that names the deciding clue,
   a changed scene and a fresh retrieval prompt. Add cumulative practice after the first
   three units and an exit check after the sixth.
2. Pilot Lesson 6 and Lesson 15 early to check grammar interactions and persistent passage
   display. Keep passage evidence visible in review as well as in the lesson.
3. Finish Part 1. Then build Part 2 in order and Part 3 after its grammar prerequisites.
   Each unit gets four learned points, two or three visual story cards and five to seven
   varied exercises. Track the misconception each plausible wrong answer represents.
4. Review answer keys and distractors against named dictionary or grammar references.
   Use original situations. Flag any prompt with multiple defensible answers and fix it
   before release. Keep stable ids from the first publication onward.
5. Test immediate transfer and seven-day recall separately. For vocabulary review, change
   the situation while preserving the word sense. Track first attempts separately from
   retries. Run the lesson audit, full checks and browser playthroughs in both app language
   settings before catalog release.

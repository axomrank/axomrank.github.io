# Physical and Indian Geography Foundations

**Status · 2026-11-05.** All ten missions and the atlas challenge are included in the public release. Proposed home
under Geography alongside Assam Geography. Intended for a beginner with no
previous geography course and for general exam-prep revision. English and
Assamese ship together after editorial review.

## Course promise and prerequisites

Build a mental map of India and use basic Earth processes to explain what
appears on it. By the end, a learner can find a place from coordinates, read a
legend and scale, distinguish weather from climate, trace a river basin,
recognise the major physical regions of India and support an explanation with
two pieces of evidence.

- **Learner requirements:** basic reading in either language, whole-number
  arithmetic and a phone or computer. Teach compass direction, coordinates,
  scale conversions, graphs and key vocabulary in the course. No GIS,
  printed atlas, algebra, external reading or Assam Geography prerequisite.
- **Opening diagnostic:** four unscored prompts on orientation, flow,
  interpreting a chart and making a claim from map evidence. Give brief
  feedback and point learners to the relevant mission. Use parallel prompts
  after the final challenge so gains can be seen by skill.
- **Learning sequence:** Missions 1–5 introduce physical processes. Missions
  6–10 apply them to India, with Assam as a familiar anchor when it helps.
  Link out to the [Assam Geography course](ASSAM-GEOGRAPHY-COURSE.md) for the
  detailed regional treatment. In relief practice, distinguish the textbook
  Malda fault from the wider Garo–Rajmahal gap.
- **Scope boundary:** India includes location, neighbours, relief, drainage,
  monsoon, soils, vegetation, agriculture and an introduction to settlement.
  Offer extended lists of capitals, peaks, passes, projects, mineral belts,
  industries and ports as optional references. Leave detailed world regional
  geography and full economic geography for separate courses.

| **Elapsed, single sitting** | | **290 min / 4 h 50 min** |
| Delayed recall | 5 min each on days 1, 3 and 7 after finishing | 15 min separately |

At 4–6 minutes per unit with the other slots held constant, the estimated
range is 230–310 minutes active or **4 h 10 min–5 h 30 min including
breaks**. Longer retries or revisits can add time. For shorter sittings, offer
five sessions of about 50–65 minutes, with two missions per session and the
diagnostic or capstone in the first or last session. Five minutes per unit
is an estimate derived from the Axomrank design target and the Assam course
budget. There is no measured learner duration for this course.

The authoring budget is 80–120 story cards, 200–280 unit exercises and
50 test questions. Each unit gets four learned points. Allocate some to
retrieval so every unit introduces only a small amount of new material.
A provisional production estimate for one author is **54–82 working hours**
after scope agreement. Budget 8–12 for evidence and glossary, 30–44 for
bilingual content, 10–16 for visuals and integration, and 6–10 for verification.
External editorial turnaround and learner recruitment are separate.

## Unit map

Each numbered item within a mission is one unit. The practice column gives
the signature interaction and the thinking it should elicit. The mission
test mixes current material with an earlier cue where available.

| Mission | Four units | Signature practice |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Find your bearings** | 1 Compass and map legends. 2 Latitude, longitude, equator, prime meridian and hemispheres. 3 Scale, distance and direction. 4 Contours and elevation. | Tap a coordinate, measure a short route from a bar scale, then match contour spacing to steep or gentle slopes. |
| **2. Read a moving Earth** | 1 Rotation and day/night. 2 Tilt, revolution and seasons. 3 Crust, mantle and core. 4 Plate boundaries, earthquakes and volcanoes. | Compare two sunlight diagrams, match arrows to boundary types and explain why a mapped earthquake belt is a clue. |
| **3. See land take shape** | 1 Minerals, three rock families and one rock-cycle pathway. 2 Weathering, erosion, transport and deposition. 3 River valleys, meanders and floodplains. 4 Glacial, wind and coastal landforms through three contrasts. | Order a sediment journey, compare two landscape views, and match an unfamiliar profile to its dominant process. |
| **4. Follow air and fresh water** | 1 Weather, climate, basic atmosphere and observations. 2 Uneven heating, pressure and wind. 3 Evaporation, condensation, precipitation and runoff. 4 Relief rainfall, rain shadow and reading a climate chart. | Read a dated chart, trace moist air over a ridge and distinguish one storm from a long-term pattern. |
| **5. Read the ocean and hazards** | 1 Oceans, continental shelf and salinity at recognition level. 2 Waves, tides and currents. 3 Hazard, exposure and risk through flood and landslide examples. 4 Tropical cyclone, storm surge and earthquake-generated tsunami contrasts. | Match observations to ocean motions, then separate a physical hazard from the roads or settlements exposed to it. |
| **6. Put India on the map** | 1 India in South Asia, neighbouring countries and adjacent seas. 2 States and union territories through a few regional anchors. 3 Tropic of Cancer, standard meridian and simple local solar time versus IST. 4 Mainland coasts and island groups. | Tap a location, use longitude to infer which place sees sunrise earlier in a simplified example, and distinguish state outlines from physical regions. |
| **7. Cross India’s physical regions** | 1 Himalayas and eastern hill ranges. 2 Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra plains and the Thar. 3 Peninsular plateau, Ghats and the northeastern plateau connection. 4 Coastal plains and contrasting island settings. | Match cross-sections to regions, follow an evidence-based transect, and place the Garo–Rajmahal gap in the larger relief map. |
| **8. Trace India’s rivers** | 1 Basin, divide, tributary and mouth recap. 2 Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra systems. 3 Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri and Mahanadi alongside Narmada and Tapi. 4 Deltas, estuaries and comparing river profiles. | Trace one basin from headwaters to outlet, sort selected rivers by drainage direction, then identify a delta or estuary from shape and flow clues. |
| **9. Read India’s monsoon** | 1 Seasonal cycle and the distinction between weather and climate. 2 Southwest monsoon advance, Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal pathways as a teaching schematic. 3 Regional rainfall contrasts using terrain and station normals. 4 Withdrawal and the role of the northeast monsoon on the southeast coast. | Sequence a seasonal map, compare two station charts with the same normal period, and correct a claim that treats a normal date as a guarantee. |
| **10. Connect land and livelihoods** | 1 Major soil examples and soil conservation. 2 Natural vegetation and conservation across a wet-to-dry comparison. 3 Rainfall, soil and irrigation in contrasting crop choices. 4 Population density, settlements and routes with a dated map. | Match soil and vegetation clues, interpret a small official crop table, and explain a settlement pattern from a transport layer plus an independent physical layer. |

### Final atlas challenge

Use a new, clearly labelled teaching map of an Indian coastal basin with a
relief profile, seasonal rainfall chart and two road routes. Ask learners to
orient the map, trace drainage, interpret rainfall, and explain which route
meets a stated access criterion. Include a direct evidence prompt for each
part. Use the same rubric for English and Assamese and score location,
interpretation and explanation separately. Keep the four opening prompts
parallel in difficulty. Publish an answer key that cites the exact map
layers, chart values or rule behind each answer.

### Variety and review

- Start with an observable question such as “Why is this side of the ridge
  drier?” Use two or three short visual cards to give the learner evidence.
  Reuse a map across units with fewer labels as learners gain fluency.
- Use existing map, picture, match, order, spot, inline and true/false
  interactions. Give reasons after each response. Aim for three or more
  exercise formats per unit and at least one evidence-reading question in
  each lesson test. Vary answer positions and use only plausible distractors.
- Use the existing 80% first-attempt gate. A five-question test passes at
  four correct first attempts. Normal lesson retries are practice. Tests
  taken through test-out or checkpoint paths have no retries.
- Offer three short fresh-cue checks after completion. Day 1 recalls a
  location and a process, day 3 interprets a new river or climate view, and
  day 7 combines two layers in a transfer question. Start with the established
  worksheet and step-based review. Keep optional manual recall results
  separate from app completion. Claim delayed mastery only if later data
  support it.

## Build requisites and acceptance

1. **Source and map ledger.** Record a passage or data row for each tested
   claim. For each asset record source, rights, edition or dates, geometry
   simplification, attribution and language labels. Separate observed data
   from teaching schematics. Link internal notes to permanent IDs. Before
   publication resolve the rights of each map and media file used.
2. **Bilingual glossary.** Lock terms and spelling variants for contours,
   latitude, relief, weathering, erosion, drainage divide, estuary, rain
   shadow, vegetation and exposure. Check Assamese map labels and text
   together. Provide legends, accessible text equivalents, labelled units,
   and visible hit targets that work on a small phone.
3. **Two-mission pilot.** Build Mission 1 and Mission 8 first in a private
   draft, with a refresher link between map literacy and basin practice.
   That tests coordinate/contour cards, river-layer reading, explanation
   feedback and delayed review before the full asset set is made.
4. **Timing measurement.** Aim for 8–12 beginner pilot participants across
   both languages. Time from opening each mission through its first normal
   test. Record time for cards, questions, feedback and retries separately,
   with breaks excluded from active time. Report sample size, median and a
   slower observation or upper quartile for each language. Track completion
   and engagement separately from first-attempt accuracy and delayed recall.
   Re-estimate the full path from measured unit and test times before
   publishing a duration claim.
5. **Production order.** Finish the physical-process foundations, then the
   Indian applications and capstone. Use new permanent IDs for each
   question. Keep the course private during authoring. Validate content with
   `python3 scripts/build.py` and `scripts/audit_lessons.py`. Play the
   normal bilingual path with `scripts/autoplay.js` and do manual phone
   checks for chart labels, map tap targets, evidence text and gate
   behaviour. Check factual sources and Assamese editorial status
   separately from automated verification.

## Implementation and learner pilot

The course opens at `index.html?course=geography-foundations` on
the local Axomrank server. The original two-mission timing slice covers
bearings and rivers, with a contour refresher in the first river unit.
It has eight units and remains the basis of the 50-minute timing protocol.
For that protocol, complete bearings, then open
`lesson.html?id=gf-08-indian-rivers&all` to skip the added missions.
Use a clean profile and complete the river units in order before the test.

The full draft uses the planned ten-mission sequence. It contains
40 units, 80 story cards, 200 practice questions and ten five-question
tests. The linked [atlas worksheet](../content/geography-foundations-atlas.html)
contains the opening diagnostic, final field case and day 1, 3 and 7
recall checks. The worksheet uses manual scoring and keeps the recall
results separate from course progress. Rivers has returned to position 8
with its existing lesson, unit and question IDs.

The build and all ten lesson audits passed. Chromium autoplay completed
the normal path at 390 × 844 in both languages, loaded the 28 course
illustrations and opened the worksheet from the course page. On each
mission test, 3/5 first attempts failed despite successful retries and 4/5
passed. A lesson test-out did not retry missed questions.

The original timing slice has eight original teaching visuals. The compass, grid, scale
and contour figures are synthetic practice maps. The basin and mouth
figures are schematic. The national river views use the repository’s
DataMeet and Natural Earth outline, with manually drawn general directions
checked against the [CWC basin map and table](https://cwc.gov.in/sites/default/files/preliminary-report-final.pdf)
and [receiving-sea table](https://cwc.gov.in/en/file/29383/download?token=wJhOuXV2).
They simplify channels and omit basin boundaries. Reuse terms and an
appropriate national river layer still need review for publication.
Learners can reveal a bilingual text description on image-based questions.

The two added missions use eight original schematics. Rotation and tilt
use [NASA Space Place](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/seasons/en/). Interior
and boundary diagrams use [USGS Earth structure](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/inside.html)
and [plate motions](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/understanding.html).
Rock and sediment panels use the [USGS rock cycle](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1994/0636/report.pdf)
and [sediment process guide](https://pubs.usgs.gov/wri/1999/4227/report.pdf).
The bend panel uses [USGS meander evidence](https://www.usgs.gov/educational-resources/find-feature-meander).
The three profiles illustrate processes described by the National Park
Service for [glacial valleys](https://www.nps.gov/articles/ushapedvalleysfjordshangingvalleys.htm)
and [coastal landforms](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/geology/coastal-landforms.htm).
Their shapes are teaching examples. The profile mission links back to the
contour unit.

each participant. Use a clean profile and the learner’s preferred language.
Invite beginners to work at their own pace. Observe whether the evidence
